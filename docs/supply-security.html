Security of supply

Switzerland imports all its oil and gas. This page shows what is held in reserve (mandatory stocks of fuels), how oil stocks changed, and how gas arrives and is stored abroad. Electricity reserves are on the Electricity page (storage lakes: Renewables).

Oil and fuels

The Confederation orders companies to hold mandatory stocks (Pflichtlager): motor petrol, diesel and heating oil for 4.5 months of average demand, jet fuel for 3 months. The stocks belong to about 280 companies. The target is the average sales of the last three years. At the end of 2025, heating oil, petrol and diesel were above their targets (1,235, 1,117 and 1,239 thousand m³ against 863, 1,078 and 1,198) and jet fuel was 26 % below (368 against 497). Stocks were last released in part in 2022 (the report of 2023 lists 175,000 m³ of diesel and 188,000 m³ of heating oil); in autumn 2026 releases of up to 4 % of diesel and 6.5 % of jet fuel were approved.

Natural gas

Switzerland has no large gas storage. Mandatory stocks of gas are held as heating oil, which customers with dual-fuel boilers switch to in a shortage. In a gas shortage, the first measure is to cut demand. Winter gas is covered by imports and by storage in neighbouring countries, so the fill level of that storage is a useful indicator.

Where the gas is stored

The storage in the four neighbouring countries sits in old gas fields, porous rock (aquifers) and salt caverns. Most of it is far from Switzerland: the large German sites (Rehden, Etzel, Epe) are in the north-west, and the French aquifers are around Paris and in the Loire valley. Closest to Switzerland are Étrez near Geneva (salt caverns), the Bavarian fields south-east of Munich, Haidach and 7Fields near Salzburg, and the fields of the Po valley.

  • Known: the fill level and the gas stored per country (chart above, weekly); the name, operator and type of each site reported to AGSI+; the total capacity per country (about 250 TWh in Germany, 200 TWh in Italy, 125 TWh in France, 100 TWh in Austria).
  • Approximate: the positions on the map. AGSI+ gives no coordinates, so each site is placed at the nearby town by hand.
  • Not on this page: capacity and fill level per site. AGSI+ publishes them per facility or pool, but only through an API that needs a free personal key; Germany's mining authority (LBEG) lists the working gas volume of each German site in a yearly PDF.
  • Unknown: which sites four German pools cover (they name no site), and which storage Swiss companies have booked and how much. From winter 2022/23 the Confederation required the Swiss gas industry to hold a gas reserve in storage abroad; the contracts and sites are not published.

Where and how much

  • How much: the quantities above, in m³ at three report dates. The Confederation also values the compulsory stocks of all kinds at CHF 3.6 billion at the end of 2022, of which energy CHF 2.9 billion.
  • Where: not published. Each stockholder has a contract that fixes the product, quantity and site, but the sites are not public. CARBURA runs its own tank farm company.
  • Past data: only in the annual reports of the Federal Office for National Economic Supply (FONES / BWL), as PDF. The 2024 report is not included because its table cannot be read reliably.
  • Electricity: the storage lakes hold about 8.9 TWh, see Renewables. Other reserves (winter hydro reserve, reserve power plant) are planned and not yet in the data.
