Mobility

Transport uses more than a third of Switzerland's final energy, mostly oil products. Electric cars are the main lever for road transport; energy use by mode and public transport are planned. Below the charts, a study of open EV profile data in Switzerland, Europe and worldwide.

Electric vehicles

A third of new cars now plug in, but the stock turns over slowly: most cars on the road still run on petrol or diesel. Figures are from the federal vehicle register (FSO) and the SFOE charging platform.

Study: open EV profile data

Modelling EV charging needs profiles: when cars are driven and parked, when and how much they charge, and how busy chargers are. This survey lists the open data in Switzerland, Europe and the rest of the world, plus the open generators that produce synthetic profiles, and names important data that is not open. Links and licences were checked in September 2026.

Key findings

  • Switzerland: measured open EV data is thin and fragmented: single sites (an EPFL fast charger, one Basel street charger), a cantonal fleet log without timestamps and a finished bay-occupancy pilot. SFOE's live charger status keeps no history; the only national time series is a researcher's archive of that feed. There is no open home-charging data, and the national travel survey (MZMV) needs an FSO contract. The practical inputs for modelling are ETH's synthetic profiles per municipality (2050) and per grid node (2030–2050).
  • Europe: open session data comes mainly from the UK (ChargePlace Scotland, Dundee, Perth & Kinross, Electric Nation) and Norway (SINTEF/NTNU). The continent offers aggregated profiles (DTU, Denmark) and live status feeds under the EU AFIR regulation that keep no history. The Dutch ElaadNL data, long the standard example, is no longer open. Open generators fill the gap: emobpy, SimBEV and RAMP-mobility, which covers Switzerland.
  • Rest of the world: US federal data dominates: EV WATTS (about 14 million sessions, free account), the NHTS travel survey and the EVI-Pro Lite profile tool. Large Chinese occupancy datasets are scraped from charging apps, so their rights are unclear; Korea and Jiaxing offer cleanly licensed session data. ACN-Data, often called open, is limited to research and education.
