Renewables

Hydropower still provides most of Switzerland's renewable energy. Growth now comes from solar PV, ambient heat used by heat pumps, and wood. Wind remains small.

Hydropower, snow and glaciers

Hydropower produced 37.5 TWh in 2025, 56 % of Swiss electricity: 16.3 TWh from run-of-river and 21.2 TWh from storage plants (production by technology is on the Electricity page). Much of its water comes from snow and ice melting in spring and summer. At the stations below, snow lay 15 to 30 fewer days a year in 1997–2026 than in 1961–1990. Since 1915 the Great Aletsch glacier has lost ice equal to 66 m of water over its whole area. Glacier melt adds runoff while the glaciers shrink, but that contribution will fall as they disappear.

Storage lakes and monthly production

Swiss storage lakes can hold 8.9 TWh of electricity, about 14 % of a year's consumption. They are lowest in April or May, before the melt, and fullest from September to November. How much water is left in late winter is a key indicator of supply security.

Solar PV

About 340,000 PV plants with 8.8 GW were in the register in 2026, and they produced about 8 TWh in 2025. Per existing building that is 2.8 kW on average, from 1.4 kW in Uri to 4.7 kW in Lucerne. The remaining roof and facade potential, roof by roof, is on sonnendach.ch; suitable sites for large ground-mounted plants are mapped by the Swiss SolarWind Explorer.

Wind and biomass

Wind supplied about 0.2 TWh in 2025, from the 64 turbines in the register. The plant register has no farm names, so the farms below are grouped from turbine locations.

Potential

If every suitable roof carried PV, Swiss buildings could produce about 65 TWh a year, and 86 TWh with facades: eight to eleven times today's PV production. Only about a quarter of the roof potential falls in the winter half-year; facades and alpine plants produce relatively more in winter. Estimates depend on what counts as usable: EPFL's machine-learning study of 9.6 million roof surfaces finds 24 ± 9 TWh, counting only the usable part of each roof.
