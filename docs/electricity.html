Energy communities

Swiss law lets neighbours share locally produced electricity in three forms:

  • ZEV (Zusammenschluss zum Eigenverbrauch, since 2018): owners and tenants behind one grid connection share the output of their PV system and face the grid as a single customer. The PV system must reach at least 10 % of the connection capacity (EnG Art. 17–18, EnV Art. 14–18).
  • Virtual ZEV (since 2025): the same, but the grid operator's connection lines below 1 kV can be used instead of private cabling.
  • LEG (lokale Elektrizitätsgemeinschaft, since 1 January 2026): consumers, producers and storage in the same municipality and grid area, all with smart meters, share electricity over the public grid. Local production must reach 5 % of the members' connection capacity. The electricity shared every 15 minutes gets a 40 % discount on the network tariff (20 % if it passes a transformer) (StromVG Art. 17d–17e, StromVV Art. 19e–19h).

There is no open register of ZEV or LEG. The grid operators' survey below counts the PV systems inside ZEV: about 23,700 with 770 MW at the end of 2024. Swissolar estimates about 1,860 LEG by the end of June 2026 (see Library), and Winterthur publishes the first open time series of electricity exchanged within its LEG.
